\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why simple adaptation beat the tested Transformers}

The result favours task adaptation, not a general rejection of Transformers.
The controlled distribution is narrow, training contains 160 rollouts and
4,036 usable windows, the pretrained MultiPath representation is strong, and
the explicit history covers only one second. A low-capacity head can therefore
correct a substantial domain mismatch without learning a new interaction
representation. The ablations show that \Tone{} and \Ttwo{} do use sequence
information; their lower rank is not explained by total input neglect. A
critical discussion should contrast representation sufficiency, sample
efficiency, optimisation and the scarcity of response-active cases.

\subsection{Why offline gain did not imply uniform closed-loop gain}

The planner consumes a calibrated distribution rather than ADE alone. Risk
allocation transforms it into constraints, solver feasibility limits candidate
motion, and the supervisor changes execution. The response-tail calibration
failure supplies a concrete mechanism by which excellent aggregate prediction
can coexist with conditional downstream effects. Because \Bone{} also differs
from \Bzero{} in frozen calibration, the correct conclusion concerns the
predictor stack, not neural weights in isolation.

\subsection{Adaptive risk as a frontier point}

Adaptive risk remains useful even though universal dominance is refuted. It can
move the safety--efficiency operating point, but its value depends on predictor,
target style and timing. Comparing it against aggressive, medium and
conservative fixed controls prevents a favourable single-baseline comparison
from being mistaken for general superiority. This negative result directly
refines the project's original planning hypothesis.

Two additional boundaries require explicit wording. Offline test and closed
loop share initialisation IDs 46--50; closed loop was not used for model
selection but is not an externally independent population replication. Also,
the implementation diagram describes information flow and must not be presented
as a causal graph.

\subsection{Implications and future work}

For research practice, simple adaptation should be a mandatory baseline before
claiming an interaction architecture improvement, and closed-loop evaluation
should expose policy and timing moderators rather than collapse them into one
score. For system design, calibration should be evaluated on response-active
subsets, and solver/supervisor telemetry should accompany safety--efficiency
metrics.

Future work should replicate across maps and junctions, increase independent
initialisation clusters, enrich response-active data, test subgroup-aware
calibration, run a matched calibration factorial, extend the interaction
history, and evaluate graph/attention architectures at a scale commensurate
with their capacity. Joint training of predictive uncertainty and risk
allocation is a further direction, but requires new controls to preserve causal
interpretability.
