\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In the controlled Town05 give-way distribution, task adaptation improved
prediction relative to the pretrained MultiPath control (RQ1), while the tested
Transformer adapters used their temporal inputs but added no consistent benefit
beyond simple adaptation and matched-scope MLP controls (RQ2). The verified
offline improvement did not transfer uniformly: its effect on completion and
physical separation changed with risk policy and target style (RQ3). Adaptive
risk likewise occupied useful context-dependent operating points but did not
dominate the three fixed-risk controls (RQ4). These findings are bounded to the
frozen simulator contract and do not establish real-world safety. The strongest
contribution is therefore the coupled evidence chain: a machine-learning gain is
not interpreted as a driving gain until calibration, risk allocation, solver
return status, controller acceptance/fallback, supervision and executed
geometry have been evaluated together.
