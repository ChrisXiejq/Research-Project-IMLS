\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This dissertation tested a progression from task adaptation to explicit
interaction modelling and finally to closed-loop predictor--risk coupling in a
controlled CARLA give-way scenario. Task adaptation strongly improved held-out
in-distribution prediction and was the best of the tested models. The
Transformer adapters demonstrably used their temporal input but did not surpass
the simple adapted head. More accurate offline prediction did not create a
uniform closed-loop gain: its effect changed with risk policy and target
behaviour. Adaptive risk consequently remained one context-dependent point on a
safety--efficiency frontier rather than a universal replacement for fixed risk.

The central conclusion is methodological as well as empirical: prediction
quality, calibration, risk allocation, solver feasibility and supervisor
activity must be evaluated together before an offline machine-learning gain is
interpreted as a driving benefit. The result is bounded to the declared CARLA
scenario and does not constitute a real-world safety claim.
