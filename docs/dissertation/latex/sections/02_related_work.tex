vector/agent-centric representations, transfer or task adaptation, and

\subsection{Interaction-aware and Transformer predictors}

Transformers provide content-dependent attention over a sequence
\citep{vaswani2017attention}, and interaction-prediction methods can model
temporal and inter-agent context. However, a better architecture claim needs a
matched task, data split, optimisation budget and non-attention capacity
control. The present comparison therefore pairs \BtwoM{} with \Tone{} and
\BtwoD{} with \Ttwo{}, while retaining the simpler \Bone{} adaptation.

\subsection{Prediction-aware planning and closed-loop evaluation}

Open-loop accuracy is not equivalent to downstream driving utility because a
planner reacts to the full predictive distribution and because solver and
supervisor logic can attenuate, amplify or reverse a predictor change. Review
work that trains or evaluates prediction through planning objectives, and work
that contrasts open-loop with closed-loop metrics.

\subsection{Synthesis and study positioning}

The study does not propose a universal new prediction architecture. It uses a
strictly controlled setting to test a chain of increasingly demanding claims:
task adaptation, sequence use, architecture superiority, closed-loop transfer
and risk-policy dominance. This positioning connects the machine-learning
contribution to the original planning question without attributing all
closed-loop behaviour to the supervisor.
