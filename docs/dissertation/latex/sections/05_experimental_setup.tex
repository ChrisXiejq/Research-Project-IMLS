\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Offline selection and frozen test}

The selection experiment comprises five trainable variants and three random
seeds, giving 15 validation runs. Rollout-macro negative log likelihood (NLL)
is the registered selection metric; top-1 average and final displacement error
(ADE/FDE), reactive-subset error and latency are secondary. The checkpoint
selected without test access is then evaluated once on the frozen test split,
together with the pretrained \Bzero{} control. Constant-velocity, clipped
constant-acceleration and train-mean route-prior predictors provide simple
physical baselines. This design addresses H1 and H2 without test-set tuning.

The frozen test contains 315 overlapping full-horizon windows nested within
20 rollouts and five held-out ego initialisations (46--50). Headline NLL, ADE
and FDE point estimates first average within rollout and then equally across
rollouts. Paired directional inference contributes one effect per held-out
initialisation; prediction windows are never treated as independent
replications. These groups are distinct from corrected-R3 groups 101--105 and
are not pooled with them.

\subsection{Mechanism and fairness diagnostics}

For \Tone{} and \Ttwo{}, valid interaction tokens are zeroed or shuffled while
weights remain frozen. A performance change establishes input sensitivity, not
causal interaction understanding. Raster and target-history shuffles provide
the corresponding input diagnostic for \Bone{}. Aggregate and
response-active calibration are reported separately. The training-budget audit
records trainable parameters, selected epochs and ceiling selections. These
analyses constrain interpretation of H1/H2 and cannot reselect a model.

\subsection{Corrected deployment gate}

R1 corrects the joint-mode mapping and applies one frozen minimum-acceleration
contract to fixed and adaptive risk. R2 then executes ten pilot rollouts to
test numerical stability, treatment identity, distinct-mode use and runtime.
The pilot is a deployment gate, not an effect experiment. G2 selected the
corrected prospective route before formal R3 outcomes were observed.

\subsection{Corrected formal closed-loop matrix}

The only primary corrected closed-loop experiment is
\[
2\ \text{predictor stacks}\times4\ \text{risk policies}\times
2\ \text{target styles}\times5\ \text{initialisation groups}=80
\]
rollouts. Predictors are \Bzero{} and the frozen \Bone{} stack; the latter
includes its frozen calibration, so the estimand is not a weight-only effect.
Risk treatments are fixed aggressive, fixed medium, fixed conservative and
adaptive. Target styles are assertive and reactive. Initialisation groups
101--105 continue the frozen in-distribution sampling stream without reusing
the previously opened groups 46--50. Execution is block randomised, and
transactional receipts distinguish scientific outcomes from infrastructure
failures.

H3 compares \Bone{} with \Bzero{} within each policy--style cell. A cell
supports the registered direction only when mean event-clock completion is
lower and actual-bounding-box footprint separation is no worse. H4 compares
adaptive risk with each of the three fixed controls within predictor--style
strata. Dominance requires no worse completion, no worse separation and no
excess binary scientific failure.

\subsection{Outcomes and statistical analysis}

The primary efficiency outcome is event-clock completion time. The primary
continuous safety-margin outcome is minimum separation computed from actual
actor bounding boxes. Native collision, footprint collision, fixed-geometry
yield failure and completion failure are binary guards. Solver identity, factual
controller acceptance/fallback, raw return status and supervisor activity audit
treatment implementation and mechanisms; they are not alternative primary
outcomes or certificates of mathematical feasibility.

For each formal contrast, the five paired initialisation groups are the
independent units. We report
paired directions, mean effects and deterministic cluster-bootstrap intervals.
Two-sided exact sign-flip tests are adjusted within prospectively declared
families using Holm's method. Their interpretation assumes a symmetric
distribution of paired cluster effects; treatment labels were not randomly
assigned within a pair, so they are reported as small-sample sensitivity
analyses rather than randomisation inference. With five pairs, the minimum attainable
two-sided exact value is $p=0.0625$; consequently the analysis does not claim
conventional significance or equivalence.

\subsection{Evidence hierarchy and study stop}

The R3 analysis contract, outcomes, censoring rules, multiplicity families and
study-stop rule were frozen before formal outcomes. R3 completed all 80
prespecified rollouts and passed its integrity gate, so no outcome-selected R3
expansion or R4 matrix is authorised. The sole later CARLA collection is the
externally requested, prospectively frozen SF4 mechanism ablation; its size and
stopping rule do not depend on the observed R3 direction. Earlier nominal/timing experiments are retained only as secondary
mechanism and sensitivity evidence because their control and geometry
implementation differs; their estimates are never pooled with corrected R3.

\subsection{Supervisor-feedback mechanism amendment}
\label{sec:supervisor-feedback-amendment}

After R3 and before submission, external supervisor feedback identified four
specific audit questions.  This amendment does not introduce H5 or use the
observed H3/H4 direction to enlarge R3.  First, hash-verified R3 telemetry is
reanalysed post hoc to quantify cautious approach, sustained stop position and
clearance-to-resumption event clocks.  Here stop--conflict distance is the
signed frozen-route quantity $s_{\mathrm{conflict}}-s_{\mathrm{ego}}$ from the
ego actor/reference point, not vehicle-bumper clearance; positive means the
reference point remains upstream.  Configured designed clearance is
$s_{\mathrm{conflict}}-s_{\mathrm{stop}}$, while signed stop-line error is
$s_{\mathrm{stop}}-s_{\mathrm{ego}}$: positive means stopping upstream/short
of the configured stop point and negative means passing it.  Second, the same raw logs are used to
separate control contexts without solver telemetry, rule-bypass/no-solve,
attempted-controller-accepted and attempted-fallback/nonaccepted decisions.
Finite CasADi solver-time and controller-acceptance denominators contain factual
solve attempts only; bypass requests, raw return statuses and eventual rollout
outcomes are reported separately.  The historical logger field named
``optimal'' is explicitly not treated as mathematical optimality or an MPC
feasibility certificate.  Third, the withdrawn top-probability/oracle-best-mode
hit-rate percentage is replaced throughout by rollout-macro frozen
test NLL/ADE/FDE and paired held-out-initialisation effects.

The sole additional CARLA experiment, SF4, prospectively isolates the complete
application authority of the corrected reduced-intervention rule-aware
supervisor.  This is not the historical full-supervisor configuration. Both arms use the same B1
predictor, estimator, configured rule-SMPC bypass logic, risk interface and
candidate computations.  With authority \emph{on}, requested reference
shaping, forced reference linearisation, heading cost, rule-SMPC bypass,
post-solver action replacement, release/recovery state and next-step control
history may affect execution.  With authority \emph{off}, those requests are
retained as shadow telemetry, rule-bypass requests do not skip the solve, and
the solver/control/state path is neutral except that estimator output may still
feed the adaptive-risk treatment.  Native collision monitoring remains active
in both arms.  The matrix is
\[
1\ \text{B1 stack}\times2\ \text{risk policies}\times
2\ \text{authority arms}\times2\ \text{target styles}\times
10\ \text{new init groups}=80\ \text{rollouts}.
\]
Risk compares adaptive with the original fixed-medium control shown in the
criticised baseline figure; SF4 therefore identifies authority masking for
this pair, not for the aggressive or conservative fixed operating points.
Init groups 106--115 form complete treatment blocks.  The primary mechanism estimand is
the init-level difference in differences
\[
 (\text{adaptive}-\text{fixed})_{\mathrm{authority\ on}}-
 (\text{adaptive}-\text{fixed})_{\mathrm{authority\ off}},
\]
after averaging the two target styles.  Failure-penalised completion time uses
the 30\,s horizon for a native CARLA collision or zero-margin physical
actual-bounding-box overlap, route-projected fixed-geometry yield failure, or
non-completion.  Realised-trajectory conflict geometry is sensitivity-only;
overlap after 0.25\,m inflation per actor is a separate safety-margin
diagnostic, not a physical-collision label.  Inference
uses the exact two-sided sign-flip distribution over the ten blocks and a
10,000-resample block bootstrap.  The sign-flip value is interpreted under a
symmetric cluster-effect assumption and accompanies the effect estimate and
interval rather than replacing them.  All adverse scientific outcomes are retained
and the registered 80-rollout stop rule applies irrespective of effect
direction.  Zero requested supervisor activity is retained as a scientific
first-stage result rather than an integrity failure or rerun trigger.  The
intervention identifies the complete application authority of that corrected
reduced-intervention supervisor inside the
frozen stack; prediction, interaction estimation, adaptive-risk allocation,
SMPC constraints and outcome monitoring remain present in both arms.
