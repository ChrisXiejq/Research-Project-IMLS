\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments are organised around the four hypotheses introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:introduction}. Each subsection defines its treatment, control,
independent experimental unit, primary comparison and interpretation rule.
Engineering checks such as data-integrity audits, deployment pilots and
telemetry validation are used to establish that a comparison is meaningful;
they are not presented as separate scientific experiments.

Across the closed-loop hypotheses, event-clock completion time is the primary
efficiency outcome and minimum separation between the actual CARLA actor
bounding boxes is the primary continuous safety-margin outcome. Native
collision, zero-margin footprint collision, fixed-geometry yield failure and
non-completion are binary guards. Five paired initialisation groups form the
independent units for each formal contrast. We report the paired directions,
mean effects and deterministic cluster-bootstrap intervals, with two-sided
exact sign-flip tests adjusted within the declared families using Holm's
method. Because treatment labels were not randomised within pairs, these tests
are small-sample sensitivity analyses under a symmetric paired-effect
assumption rather than randomisation inference. With five pairs, the minimum
attainable two-sided value is $p=0.0625$; the experiments therefore estimate
direction and context dependence but do not claim conventional significance
or equivalence.

\subsection{H1: task adaptation versus the pretrained predictor}

H1 asks whether adaptation to the controlled give-way distribution improves
prediction relative to the pretrained MultiPath model. The treatment is
\Bone{}, which retrains the final MultiPath prediction head, and the principal
control is the frozen pretrained \Bzero{} stack. Constant-velocity, clipped
constant-acceleration and train-mean route-prior predictors provide additional
physical controls, preventing a gain over the pretrained network alone from
being mistaken for useful trajectory prediction.

The dataset contains 200 factorial rollouts and is split by initialisation,
not by overlapping prediction window: 160 rollouts are used for training, 20
for validation and 20 for the frozen test. The 315 full-horizon test windows
are nested within the 20 test rollouts and five held-out initialisations
(46--50). Headline NLL, ADE and FDE first average valid windows within each
rollout and then weight rollouts equally. Directional inference contributes
one paired \Bone{}--\Bzero{} effect per held-out initialisation, so prediction
windows are never treated as independent replications.

Model and checkpoint selection use validation rollout-macro NLL without test
access. The selected checkpoint is opened once on the frozen test, where NLL
is the primary prediction criterion and ADE/FDE provide geometric checks. H1
is evaluated from the common frozen-test comparison, the five init-level
paired directions and performance relative to the three physical baselines.
Raster and target-history shuffles, together with aggregate and
response-active calibration analyses, explain the source and boundary of any
gain but cannot reselect the model.

\subsection{H2: Transformer adapters versus matched non-attention controls}

H2 asks whether explicit interaction-sequence modelling adds value beyond
simple task adaptation and beyond additional non-attention capacity. Five
trainable variants are run with three random seeds, giving 15 validation runs.
The mean-only comparison pairs the Transformer \Tone{} with the flattened-
sequence MLP \BtwoM{}; the distributional comparison pairs \Ttwo{} with
\BtwoD{}. Each pair shares its residual-output scope and approximately matches
adapter capacity. The simple \Bone{} model remains the reference for whether
the added adapter complexity improves the best task-adaptation result.

All variants use the same rollout-disjoint split, anchors, loss, optimiser
budget and validation ranking metric. Their validation medians determine the
frozen test checkpoint before any test metric is examined. H2 requires a
consistent advantage for the Transformer comparisons that is not reproduced
by the corresponding MLP controls and that improves upon simple adaptation.
Frozen zeroing and shuffling of valid sequence tokens test whether \Tone{} and
\Ttwo{} use their temporal inputs. Parameter counts, selected epochs and
selections at the epoch ceiling are reported as fairness constraints: these
diagnostics distinguish ``no benefit under the tested configurations'' from a
general conclusion about the Transformer architecture.

\subsection{H3: offline prediction gain versus closed-loop benefit}

H3 tests whether the offline \Bone{}--\Bzero{} advantage transfers
consistently after prediction passes through calibration, risk allocation,
optimisation and supervision. Before measuring effects, the deployment path
was corrected so that joint prediction modes were mapped consistently and one
minimum-acceleration contract was shared by fixed and adaptive risk. A
ten-rollout pilot checked numerical stability, treatment identity,
distinct-mode use and runtime. This pilot was a validity gate only; it was not
used to estimate treatment effects or select favourable outcomes.

The formal closed-loop experiment is
\[
2\ \text{predictor stacks}\times4\ \text{risk policies}\times
2\ \text{target styles}\times5\ \text{initialisation groups}=80
\]
rollouts. Predictor treatments are the frozen \Bzero{} and \Bone{} deployment
stacks. Because \Bone{} includes its validation-frozen calibration, the
estimand is the deployed stack difference rather than a neural-weight-only
effect. Risk policies are fixed aggressive, fixed medium, fixed conservative
and adaptive; target styles are assertive and reactive. Initialisation groups
101--105 are new samples from the frozen in-distribution stream and are not
pooled with offline-test groups 46--50. Complete treatment blocks are executed
in randomised order, while transactional receipts distinguish scientific
outcomes from infrastructure failures.

Within each of the eight risk-policy--target-style cells, the treatment is
\Bone{} and the paired control is \Bzero{}. A cell supports the registered
joint direction only when mean completion time is lower and mean physical
separation is no worse. In-loop prediction checks first verify that the model
manipulation remains present after deployment; they are manipulation checks,
not a second offline benchmark. H3 concerns consistency across the eight
contexts, so an isolated favourable cell is not interpreted as general
offline-to-closed-loop transfer.

\subsection{H4: adaptive risk versus the fixed-risk frontier}

H4 reuses the same blocked 80-rollout matrix but changes the scientific
contrast. Within each predictor--target-style stratum, adaptive risk is paired
separately with fixed aggressive, fixed medium and fixed conservative risk,
giving 12 prespecified comparisons. Adaptive risk empirically dominates a
fixed operating point only when it has no worse completion time, no worse
minimum physical separation and no excess binary scientific failure. Comparing
three fixed controls, rather than one convenient baseline, tests a frontier
claim and prevents superiority to one operating point from being generalised
to all fixed risk.

Solver identity, within-rollout adaptive variation, fixed-policy stability,
raw solver return status, factual controller acceptance or fallback and
supervisor activity are implementation and mechanism checks for H4. In
particular, rollout completion and a historical logger field named
``optimal'' are not treated as mathematical MPC-feasibility certificates.
Bypass/no-solve decisions, attempted-and-accepted solves and attempted-but-
nonaccepted decisions are reported with separate denominators.

External feedback also motivated one prospectively frozen mechanism audit of
whether the shared supervisor masks the adaptive-versus-fixed-medium contrast.
It crosses the \Bone{} stack with two risk policies, supervisor application
authority on/off, two target styles and ten new complete initialisation blocks,
giving 80 rollouts. Both authority arms retain the same predictor, estimator,
risk interface, SMPC constraints and native collision monitoring. The
authority-off arm records supervisor requests as shadow telemetry but prevents
them from changing the solver, control or state path. Its primary estimand is
the init-level difference in differences
\[
 (\text{adaptive}-\text{fixed medium})_{\text{authority on}}-
 (\text{adaptive}-\text{fixed medium})_{\text{authority off}},
\]
after averaging target styles. This audit identifies the application authority
of the corrected reduced-intervention supervisor for one policy pair; it does
not isolate the predictor, estimator-to-risk path or SMPC constraints, and it
does not create a fifth hypothesis or alter the already frozen H4 contrasts.

The formal outcome definitions, censoring rules, multiplicity families and
study-stop rule were fixed before the corresponding outcomes were examined.
All adverse scientific outcomes are retained. Earlier experiments using
different controller or geometry implementations are used only as labelled
mechanism or sensitivity evidence and are never pooled with the formal
closed-loop matrix.
