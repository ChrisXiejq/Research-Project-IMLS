\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Results follow H1--H4 and separate corrected primary evidence from legacy
mechanism diagnostics.

\subsection{Data and protocol integrity}

All 200 planned prediction-data rollouts completed. Raw and merged manifests
agree on 11,230 samples, rollout groups are disjoint across the 160/20/20
train/validation/test split, no duplicate keys or normalisation leakage are
detected, and the largest descriptive split-covariate standardised mean
difference is 0.208. The corrected R3 matrix subsequently completed all 80/80
prespecified rollouts and passed its analysis, integrity, stop and archive
gates.

\subsection{H1: simple task adaptation produces the reliable offline gain}

Across the three validation seeds, median macro NLL ranks \Bone{} first,
followed by \BtwoD{}, \Ttwo{}, \Tone{} and \BtwoM{}. Relative to pretrained
\Bzero{} on the frozen test, \Bone{} reduces top-1 ADE by 1.193~m, FDE by
2.555~m and uncalibrated rollout-macro NLL by 0.314~nats/step. It also improves
ADE and FDE relative to constant velocity, clipped constant acceleration and
the train-mean prior in all five test initialisation groups. H1 is therefore
supported within the frozen Town05 distribution.

\subsection{H2: tested Transformer adapters add no consistent benefit}

The paired-head comparisons point in opposite directions. \Tone{} attains a
test NLL of 2.003746 versus 2.024409 for \BtwoM{}, whereas \Ttwo{} attains
1.878148 versus 1.872789 for \BtwoD{}. Neither configuration exceeds the
simple \Bone{} adaptation. Sequence shuffling nevertheless worsens \Tone{} and
\Ttwo{} NLL, so the result is not explained by complete sequence neglect.
H2 is not supported under the tested protocol. Because the configurations are
not parameter matched and 10/15 selections occur at the epoch ceiling, this is
not evidence that Transformers are generally ineffective.

\subsection{R3 manipulation and nominal reliability}

The deployed prediction manipulation is strong: all 40/40
metric--policy--style checks favour \Bone{} over \Bzero{} in every paired
initialisation group. Across these checks, \Bone{}--\Bzero{} top-1 ADE effects
range from $-1.352$ to $-1.125$~m and FDE effects from $-3.152$ to
$-2.315$~m. These checks verify that the predictor stacks remain distinct
inside R3; they are not an independent offline benchmark.

The adaptive solver is used on every audited step in all four adaptive cells,
all 20 adaptive rollouts vary within rollout, and fixed settings are stable
across initialisations. No native collision, footprint collision,
fixed-geometry yield failure or completion failure is observed. These zero
counts establish nominal feasibility for the observed matrix but cannot prove
zero population risk or treatment equivalence.

\subsection{H3: prediction gain does not transfer uniformly}

Only two of eight policy--style cells jointly have faster mean completion and
no-worse mean footprint separation for \Bone{} relative to \Bzero{}:
adaptive/reactive and fixed-aggressive/assertive. The remaining six cells fail
at least one registered direction. For example, adaptive/assertive is
0.240~s slower with 0.0067~m lower separation, whereas
fixed-aggressive/assertive is 0.790~s faster with 0.0053~m higher separation.
All Holm-adjusted exact tests are non-confirmatory. H3 is therefore not
supported as a universal translation claim; the data do not imply that the
predictor stack has no closed-loop value.

\subsection{H4: adaptive risk is conditional, not dominant}

Adaptive risk dominates its fixed comparator in only 3/12 prespecified
predictor--style--comparator contrasts: \Bzero{}/assertive versus fixed medium,
and \Bone{}/reactive versus fixed aggressive and fixed medium. It does not
dominate in the other 9/12 contrasts. H4 is therefore not supported as a
universal dominance claim. The result retains a useful role for adaptive risk
as a context-dependent operating point rather than a universal replacement.

\subsection{Secondary mechanism and sensitivity evidence}

Input diagnostics characterise \Bone{} as raster-dominant: raster shuffling
increases ADE by approximately 0.270--0.293~m across seeds, whereas target
history shuffling changes aggregate ADE by only about 0.00008~m on average.
Legacy response-active analysis also shows that global calibration can improve
aggregate NLL while worsening a very small shifted active tail (15 windows,
six rollouts and three initialisation groups). Earlier arrival-timing logs show
that separation, solver feasibility and supervisor activity change together,
so the supervisor cannot be identified as the single cause. Finally, a
collision-filtered 15-run validation sensitivity preserves the architecture
ranking. These analyses explain boundaries and alternative explanations; they
are not pooled with corrected R3 and do not create additional hypotheses.
